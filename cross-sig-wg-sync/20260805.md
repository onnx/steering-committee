### Discussion: handling the volume of security reports

- The project receives a high volume of LLM-generated security reports. They are typically submitted with an expectation of high-severity CVEs, but most turn out to be minor issues. Triaging and fixing them is nonetheless time-consuming.
- **CVE scoring:** within the Architecture & Infra SIG there is not yet a common understanding of how to score severity — what counts as high vs. medium — and several different rule sets exist.
  - **Ask:** is there a CVE expert at Intel, Microsoft or IBM who does this regularly and could help define a rule set for scoring and publishing CVEs?
- Worth finding out how the PyTorch Foundation handles vulnerabilities across its projects (e.g. vLLM was also part of the Patch the Planet programme).
- Given the volume of researchers, bots, LLMs and agents filing issues and PRs, relying on humans alone will not scale — automation integrated into CI (human-approved at the end) is likely needed, in the same way we have CI for tests.
- Fuzzing is already integrated, but with LLM-driven reporting it is no longer sufficient on its own. An LLM-based security check in CI is an option, though nobody present has experience setting that up.
- The Architecture & Infra SIG (met the previous day) split the open issues: Javier taking some, Christian and Justin taking others. A number of PRs are being worked through to reduce already-known security issues.

## Ideas for tutorials and documentation

- A question was raised as to when we last took a pulse from the ONNX community on what is missing, particularly for tutorials — ONNX has been around a while and there is not much genuinely new that needs that level of hand-holding, so we should first understand what would drive the most value and then generate ideas against that. (A community survey was run at the beginning of the year; see the retro section above for the results and raw data.)
- With the growth of AI/agentic coding, material **targeted at agents** may now be more useful than classic tutorials — e.g. better skill files in the repos, and documentation written for consumption by agents.
- Next step: review the community survey feedback for anything related to models and tutorials, combine it with feedback gathered from the general channels, and bring it to the Steering Committee.

### MCP server as a documentation format

- For ONNX-MLIR, an MCP server is being developed to help both users and developers — e.g. answering questions like how to integrate with Hugging Face, how to obtain a model, or what the current restrictions on certain operations are.
- Idea: an MCP server as a new format for tutorials/help pages, potentially replacing or complementing static documentation.
- Alexandre is happy to share the general ideas and approach with anyone interested.
- Agreed to capture this in the backlog as a GitHub issue (possibly suitable as a good-first / community contribution). Alexandre will file the issue.
